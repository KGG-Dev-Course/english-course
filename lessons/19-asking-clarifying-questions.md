## Why this matters

Misunderstandings are expensive. If you misunderstand a requirement, you can spend a week building the wrong feature. Many non-native speakers don't ask because they're afraid of looking slow. But native speakers ask clarifying questions all the time. It's a sign of a careful developer, not a weak one.

## The core skill

### When you didn't hear or catch something

In fast meetings or bad calls, you'll miss words. Ask for a repeat, and be specific when you can.

```text
Sorry, I didn't catch that. Could you say it again?
Sorry, you cut out for a second. What was the deadline?
Could you speak a bit more slowly, please?
```

*Catch* here means "hear and understand". *Cut out* means the audio stopped on a call.

### When you heard it but don't understand

Ask about the specific part you don't understand. That's faster than "Can you explain again?"

```text
What do you mean by "real-time"? Within a second, or within a few minutes?
When you say "all users", does that include admins?
Could you give me an example?
```

### Checking your understanding

Repeat what you understood in your own words. This is called *paraphrasing*, and it's the best way to find misunderstandings early.

```text
So, if I understand correctly, we need the export by Friday, but only
for the admin dashboard, not for regular users. Is that right?

Just to confirm: the new API replaces the old one, and we remove
the old one in January?
```

### Asking about requirements

Before you start a task, these questions save the most time:

```text
What problem are we trying to solve?
Who is going to use this?
What does "done" look like?
Is there a deadline?
What should happen if the data is missing?
```

These are especially useful when a ticket has only one line.

## Useful phrases

**Asking for a repeat**

- **Sorry, I didn't catch** the last part.
- **Could you repeat** the version number?
- **Sorry, you broke up** there. (= the audio was bad)

**Asking for meaning**

- **What do you mean by** "soon"?
- **Could you clarify** what "fast" means here?
- **Could you give me an example?**

**Paraphrasing**

- **So, if I understand correctly,** …
- **Just to confirm,** …
- **Let me make sure I got this right:** …
- **So what you're saying is** …

**Checking details**

- **Does that include** mobile users?
- **What should happen if** the payment fails?
- **Who should I ask about** the design?

## Common mistakes

**"Can you repeat again?"**
*Repeat* already means "say again", so *again* is extra: *"Can you **repeat that**?"*

**"What means 'idempotent'?"**
In questions with *what* as the object, you need *does*: *"What **does** 'idempotent' **mean**?"*

**"Sorry, I didn't understand nothing."**
English uses only one negative here: *"Sorry, I didn't understand **anything**."* Even better, say which part: *"Sorry, I didn't understand the part about the tokens."*

**Saying "yes" when you didn't understand.**
This is the most expensive mistake. If you're not sure, say: *"**I'm not sure I followed. Could you explain the last part again?**"*

## Formal vs. casual

- **Meeting with a client:** "Just to make sure I've understood correctly: you'd like users to receive the report every Monday, and only managers should be able to see the totals. Is that right?"
- **Chat with a teammate:** "So reports every Monday, managers only for totals, right?"

## Exercise

Take a ticket or task from your current work. Write:

1. Three clarifying questions you could ask about it.
2. One paraphrase that starts with *"So, if I understand correctly, …"*

Then actually send one of your questions to the person who wrote the ticket.

*Hint:* look for vague words like *fast, simple, all, soon* or *real-time*. They are usually the best things to ask about.

## Recap

- Ask for a repeat with *Sorry, I didn't catch that.*
- Ask about the exact word or part you don't understand.
- Paraphrase to confirm: *So, if I understand correctly…*
- Ask about the problem, the users and what "done" means.
- Never say "yes" when you didn't understand.
