## Why this matters

Chat is for your team, but email is still how developers talk to clients, recruiters, vendors and other departments. These readers often don't know you, so your email is their first impression. A clear structure makes you look professional, even if your English isn't perfect.

## The core skill

### The structure

A work email has five parts:

1. **Subject line:** says what the email is about.
2. **Greeting:** *Hi Maria,* or *Dear Mr. Lopez,*
3. **Purpose:** why you're writing, in the first sentence.
4. **Details:** only what the reader needs.
5. **Next step and closing:** what should happen next, then *Best regards,* and your name.

```text
Subject: API access for the Acme integration

Hi Maria,

I'm writing to ask for API credentials for our integration with Acme.
We're planning to start development on October 14.

We need access to the sandbox environment only. The integration will
read order data and won't write anything.

Could you send the credentials by the end of next week? Let me know if
you need any more information from our side.

Best regards,
Tomás Silva
Backend Developer, ShopFlow
```

### Write a useful subject line

The subject should tell the reader what the email is about before they open it. Include a key word and, if needed, an action.

- Weak: *Question*
- Better: *Question about the invoice API rate limits*
- With an action: *Action needed: confirm release date by Friday*

### Purpose in the first sentence

Don't start with a long introduction. Say why you're writing:

```text
I'm writing to let you know that the deployment is moved to Thursday.
I'm following up on our call about the data export.
I'd like to ask about the status of ticket #4410.
```

### End with a clear next step

Tell the reader exactly what you need and when. Then use a standard closing.

```text
Could you confirm by Wednesday whether the new date works for your team?

Best regards,
Ana
```

*Best regards*, *Kind regards* and *Thanks* are safe in almost any work email. *Cheers* is friendly and common in British and Australian English.

## Useful phrases

**Opening**

- **I'm writing to** ask about…
- **I'm following up on** my email from last week.
- **Thank you for** your quick reply.

**Giving information**

- **Please find attached** the test report. (formal)
- **I've attached** the logs. (neutral)
- **Just to give you some context,** we migrated to AWS last month.

**Asking for action**

- **Could you please** send the credentials?
- **Please let me know** if you have any questions.
- **I'd appreciate it if you could** reply by Friday.

**Closing**

- **Best regards,** / **Kind regards,** / **Thanks,**
- **Looking forward to hearing from you.**

## Common mistakes

**"I hope you're doing good."**
In standard written English, *good* describes things and *well* describes how someone is: *"I hope you're doing **well**."*

**"I want that you send me the logs."**
English doesn't use *want that*. Use *want* + person + *to*: *"I'd like you **to send** me the logs."* Or, more politely: *"Could you send me the logs?"*

**"Waiting for your reply."**
This sounds impatient and is missing a subject. Use: *"**I look forward to** your reply."*

**"Please revert back to me."**
In many countries *revert* means "reply", but in British and American English it means "go back to an old state", which developers use for code. Write: *"Please **get back to me**."* or *"Please **reply** when you can."*

## Formal vs. casual

- **To a client or someone you don't know:** "Dear Ms. Chen, I'm writing to inform you that the scheduled maintenance has been moved to Saturday, October 18. We apologize for any inconvenience. Kind regards, Tomás Silva"
- **To a colleague in another department:** "Hi Lisa, quick update: we've moved the maintenance to Saturday the 18th. Let me know if that's a problem. Thanks, Tomás"

## Exercise

Write an email to a person outside your team (a client, a vendor or another department). Ask them for something you really need for your work: access, information, a decision or a meeting.

Use the five parts: subject, greeting, purpose, details, next step and closing. Keep it under 120 words.

*Hint:* read only your subject and first sentence. Does the reader already know what you want?

## Recap

- Use a specific subject line.
- Say your purpose in the first sentence: *I'm writing to…*
- Keep details short, and end with a clear next step and deadline.
- Close with *Best regards*, *Kind regards* or *Thanks*.
- Say *get back to me*, not *revert back to me*.
