## Why this matters

Developers get more requests than they can finish: new features, "quick" fixes, extra meetings, tight deadlines. If you say yes to everything, you'll miss deadlines and burn out. Saying no clearly and politely is a professional skill, and good managers respect it.

## The core skill

### The structure: acknowledge, explain, offer

A polite *no* has three parts:

1. **Acknowledge** the request. Show you understand it's important.
2. **Explain** why you can't, in one sentence.
3. **Offer** an alternative.

```text
I understand this feature is important for the demo.
Unfortunately, I'm already working on the security fix, which has to
ship this week.
I could start on the feature on Monday, or Jun might have time sooner.
```

The alternative is the most important part. It changes your answer from "no" to "not like this, but like this".

### Pushing back on a deadline

*Push back* means to resist or disagree with a request or plan. When a deadline isn't realistic, explain the trade-off. A trade-off is what you lose when you gain something else.

```text
We can deliver by Friday, but only without the export feature.
If we need everything, I think the end of next week is more realistic.
Which is more important to you?
```

Asking "Which is more important?" gives the decision back to the person who owns the priorities.

### Saying no to the solution, not the person

Sometimes you disagree with how something should be built. Focus on the problem, and use *I* or *we*, not *you*.

- Sounds personal: *You're asking for something that will break the database.*
- Focuses on the problem: *I'm worried this could overload the database. Could we add pagination first?*

### Using "unfortunately" and "I'm afraid"

These words prepare the listener for bad news. They make your *no* sound polite.

```text
Unfortunately, we can't change the API before the release.
I'm afraid I won't be able to join the meeting tomorrow.
```

*I'm afraid* here doesn't mean you feel fear. It's a polite way to introduce bad news.

## Useful phrases

**Saying no**

- **I'm afraid I can't** take this on right now. (*take on* = accept a task)
- **Unfortunately, I don't have the capacity** this sprint.
- **I'd love to help, but** I'm fully booked until Thursday.

**Explaining**

- **I'm currently focused on** the release.
- **If I take this on, the** migration **will slip**. (*slip* = be late)

**Offering alternatives**

- **What I can do is** look at it next week.
- **Would it work if** I gave you a smaller version first?
- **Could we** move it to the next sprint?
- **Maybe** Ravi could help. **He knows** that code well.

**Pushing back**

- **I see your point, but** I have some concerns about the timeline.
- **What's the priority here?** Speed or completeness?
- **That's not realistic** for Friday, **but we could** ship a first version.

## Common mistakes

**"No, I can't."**
Correct, but very direct. Add a reason and an alternative: *"**Sorry,** I can't **this week. I could do it on Monday.**"*

**"It's impossible."**
This sounds final and negative. Explain what *is* possible: *"**That's difficult for Friday, but we could** ship the core part by then."*

**"I'm afraid I can't finish it until the deadline."**
Use *by* for the last possible moment. *Until* is for something that continues up to a time: *"I'm afraid I can't finish it **by** the deadline."*

**"Sorry, sorry, I'm really sorry, but maybe I can't do it, sorry."**
Too many apologies make you sound guilty. One is enough: *"**Sorry,** I can't do it this week."*

## Formal vs. casual

- **To a stakeholder in another department:** "Thank you for the request. Unfortunately, our team is fully committed to the Q4 release. We could consider this for the January planning. Would that work for you?"
- **To your manager:** "I can take it, but then the migration will slip by a week. Which should I prioritize?"
- **To a teammate:** "Can't today, sorry, I'm deep in the release. Tomorrow morning?"

## Exercise

Think of a request you got recently that you didn't really have time for. Write the reply you *should* have sent, using acknowledge, explain and offer.

Then write a short message pushing back on a deadline, with a clear trade-off and a question about priorities.

*Hint:* check your message for *you* sentences. Can you rewrite them with *I* or *we*?

## Recap

- Acknowledge, explain, then offer an alternative.
- Use *unfortunately* and *I'm afraid* to introduce bad news politely.
- Explain trade-offs and ask about priorities.
- Focus on the problem, not the person.
- Apologize once, not many times.
