## Why this matters

Many developers who write excellent English stay silent in meetings. The conversation moves fast, and by the time you've prepared your sentence, the topic has changed. But meetings are where decisions are made. If you don't speak, your ideas and concerns are not part of the decision.

## The core skill

### Prepare before the meeting

Read the agenda and write down one or two things you want to say. Prepare the first sentence. It's the hardest part.

```text
Agenda item: "Q4 roadmap"
My point: the search feature needs a backend change first.
First sentence: "I'd like to add something about the search feature."
```

### Get into the conversation

You don't need to wait for a long silence. Use a short phrase to show you want to speak. This gives you a moment to start.

```text
Can I add something here?
Sorry, can I just jump in? (jump in = join a conversation suddenly)
I have a quick point about that.
```

In video calls, you can also use the "raise hand" button, or write in the chat: *"I have a question about the timeline when there's a moment."*

### Interrupt politely

Sometimes you need to interrupt, for example when someone is moving on but you have an important point. Apologize briefly, then speak.

```text
Sorry to interrupt, but before we move on, I think we're missing
something about the API limits.
```

If someone interrupts you, you can continue politely:

```text
Sorry, can I just finish my point?
Just one more thing, and then I'm done.
```

### Keep it short and clear

Say your main point first, then one reason. Long explanations lose people.

```text
I think we should delay the release by a week.
The payment tests are still failing, and fixing them will take a few days.
```

At the end, you can make it clear you're done: *"That's all from me."* or *"What do you think?"*

### Buying time

If someone asks you a question and you need a moment, use a phrase that gives you time to think. It sounds much better than silence.

```text
That's a good question. Let me think about it for a second.
I'm not sure off the top of my head. I'll check and get back to you.
```

*Off the top of my head* means "without checking or thinking for long".

## Useful phrases

**Starting**

- **Can I add something** here?
- **I'd like to say something about** the timeline.
- **Building on what Sara said,** … (*build on* = add to someone's idea)

**Interrupting**

- **Sorry to interrupt, but** …
- **Can I just jump in** for a second?
- **Before we move on,** I have a quick question.

**Holding the floor** (continuing when someone interrupts)

- **Sorry, let me just finish** this point.
- **Just one more thing.**

**Finishing**

- **That's all from me.**
- **What does everyone think?**

## Common mistakes

**"Can I say one thing?" (then a five-minute speech)**
If you promise one thing, keep it short. Start with the main point: *"Can I add one thing? **I think we need more time for testing.**"*

**"Sorry for interrupt."**
After *for*, use the *-ing* form: *"Sorry for **interrupting**."* Or: *"Sorry **to** interrupt."*

**"I want to add something about what said Tom."**
In a normal statement, the subject comes before the verb: *"…about what **Tom said**."*

**Staying silent because your English isn't perfect.**
Nobody expects perfect English. A short, clear point with a small mistake is much better than a perfect point you never say.

## Formal vs. casual

- **Large meeting with leadership:** "Excuse me, could I add one point before we move on? I'm concerned that the timeline doesn't include time for the security review."
- **Team meeting:** "Sorry, quick thing before we move on: did we plan time for the security review?"

## Exercise

Look at your calendar and choose your next meeting. Before it:

1. Write down one point you want to make.
2. Prepare your first sentence, using a phrase from this lesson.
3. Say it out loud three times.

In the meeting, say it. Afterwards, write down how it went and one phrase you want to use next time.

*Hint:* the first time is the hardest. Choose a small meeting with people you know well.

## Recap

- Prepare your point and your first sentence before the meeting.
- Use short phrases to get into the conversation: *Can I add something?*
- Interrupt politely: *Sorry to interrupt, but…*
- Main point first, then one reason.
- A small mistake is better than silence.
