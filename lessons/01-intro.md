## Why this matters

The first thing you say to a new team, a recruiter or an interviewer is your introduction. A clear one makes people remember you, and it's the easiest part of any conversation to prepare in advance.

## The basic structure

A good introduction answers three questions, in this order:

1. **Who are you?** Your name and your role.
2. **What do you do?** Your main skills or the kind of work you do.
3. **What's next?** Why you're here, or what you're looking for.

```text
Hi, I'm Alex. I'm a full-stack developer.
I mostly work with React and Node.js, and I've been building web apps for five years.
I just joined the payments team, and I'm looking forward to working with you all.
```

## Useful phrases

**Your role**

- I'm a **backend / frontend / full-stack developer**.
- I work **as** a software engineer **at** a fintech company.
- I'm **currently** working on the mobile app.

**Your experience**

- I've been working with Python **for** three years.
- I've been a developer **since** 2021.
- I **specialize in** APIs and databases.

**Closing**

- I'm looking forward to working with you.
- Feel free to reach out if you have any questions.
- Nice to meet you all.

## Common mistakes

**"I am working as developer."**
You need an article before a job title: *"I work as **a** developer."*

**"I work with Python since three years."**
For something that started in the past and is still true, use the present perfect, with *for* before a length of time:
*"I've **been working** with Python **for** three years."*

**"I am in charge in the backend."**
The preposition is *of*: *"I'm in charge **of** the backend."*

## Formal vs. casual

- **Interview:** "Good morning. My name is Alex Kim, and I'm a full-stack developer with five years of experience."
- **Team chat:** "Hey everyone! I'm Alex, the new full-stack dev on the team."

Match your tone to the situation. Interviews are formal, while team chats are usually relaxed.

## Exercise

Write two introductions for yourself:

1. **Interview version** (3–4 sentences): your name, your role, your years of experience, one strength.
2. **Team chat version** (1–2 sentences): friendly and short.

*Hint:* say each one out loud. If you run out of breath, the sentence is too long.

## Recap

- Introductions follow three steps: who you are, what you do, what's next.
- Use **a/an** before a job title: *a developer, an engineer*.
- Use the present perfect with **for/since** for experience: *I've been working… for 3 years*.
- Change your tone for formal and casual situations.
