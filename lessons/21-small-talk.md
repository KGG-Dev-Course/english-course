## Why this matters

Small talk is light, friendly conversation about everyday topics. It happens in the first minutes of a call, in the kitchen, and at team events. It feels unimportant, but it builds trust. People help, promote and recommend colleagues they know as people, not only as names in a ticket.

## The core skill

### Safe topics

Good small talk topics are easy and positive:

- The weekend and plans
- Weather (especially if you're in different places)
- Food, travel, hobbies
- Light work topics: a conference, a new tool, a big release

Avoid politics, religion, salary and personal problems with colleagues you don't know well.

### Ask open questions

Questions with *what, how* and *where* keep the conversation going. Yes/no questions often end it.

- Closed: *Did you have a good weekend?* "Yes." (end)
- Open: *What did you get up to at the weekend?* "We went hiking near…"

*Get up to* is a casual phrasal verb meaning "do".

### Answer with a bit more

When someone asks you a question, don't answer with one word. Give a short answer, add one detail, then ask back.

```text
A: How was your weekend?
B: Pretty good, thanks! I finally tried that new ramen place near the
   office. It was great. How about you? Did you do anything fun?
```

That's the pattern: **answer, detail, question back.** It keeps the conversation balanced.

### Starting and ending a call

Remote teams often have one or two minutes of small talk at the start of a call while people join.

```text
Hi Lucas! How's it going? Is it still snowing in Toronto?
```

To move from small talk to work, use a friendly transition:

```text
Anyway, should we get started?
OK, I think everyone's here. Let's begin.
```

To end small talk in person, give a reason and be friendly:

```text
Well, I should get back to that bug. Nice chatting with you!
```

## Useful phrases

**Starting**

- **How's it going?** / **How are things?**
- **How was your weekend?**
- **Any plans for the weekend?**
- **How's the weather over there?**

**Keeping it going**

- **Oh, really? How was it?**
- **That sounds great / fun / tough.**
- **I've never been there. What's it like?**
- **How about you?**

**Work-related small talk**

- **Are you going to** the conference next month?
- **How's the new project going?**
- **Busy week?**

**Ending**

- **Anyway, I'll let you get back to work.**
- **Nice chatting with you!**
- **Have a good weekend!** / **Enjoy your holiday!**

## Common mistakes

**"Fine." (and nothing else)**
A one-word answer can sound unfriendly. Add a detail and ask back: *"**Good, thanks! Busy with the release, but it's going well. How about you?**"*

**"How was your weekend?" "It was very funny."**
*Funny* means it made you laugh. For a good time, use *fun*: *"It was **fun**."*

**"What did you do in the weekend?"**
Use *at* (British English) or *on* (American English): *"What did you do **at** / **on** the weekend?"*

**"How are you?" (followed by a full description of your health)**
At work, *How are you?* is usually a greeting, not a real question. A short answer is expected: *"**Good, thanks. And you?**"*

## Formal vs. casual

- **With a senior manager or client before a meeting:** "Good morning, Ms. Park. Did you have a good trip to Berlin?"
- **With a teammate:** "Hey! How was Berlin? Did you try the currywurst?"

The topic can be the same. The difference is the greeting, the name and how relaxed your words are.

## Exercise

Prepare small talk for your next call or office day:

1. Write three open questions you could ask a colleague.
2. Write your own answer to "How was your weekend?" using answer, detail, question back.
3. Write one sentence to move from small talk to work.

Then use at least one of them this week.

*Hint:* listen for details in other people's answers. A follow-up question about something they said ("Oh, how was the concert?") shows you were really listening.

## Recap

- Choose safe, positive topics.
- Ask open questions with *what, how, where*.
- Answer, add a detail, then ask back.
- Use a friendly transition from small talk to work.
- *Fun* means enjoyable; *funny* means it made you laugh.
