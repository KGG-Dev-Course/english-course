## Why this matters

In many languages, a direct request is normal and polite. In English, especially in workplaces in the US and UK, the same request can sound rude or angry. Softening language is a set of small words and structures that makes your messages sound friendly, without changing their meaning.

## The core skill

### Questions instead of orders

The simplest way to soften a request is to turn it into a question.

- Direct: *Review my PR.*
- Softer: *Can you review my PR?*
- Softer still: *Could you review my PR when you have a moment?*

*Could* and *would* are more polite than *can* and *will*. The extra phrase *when you have a moment* gives the other person some control over their time.

### Polite structures for bigger requests

For a request that takes more time or effort, use one of these:

```text
Would you mind looking at the deploy logs? (+ verb-ing)
Do you think you could help me with the migration?
I was wondering if you could join the planning meeting tomorrow.
```

*Would you mind…?* means "Is it a problem for you to…?" So the polite answer for "yes, I'll do it" is *No, not at all.*

*I was wondering if…* uses the past tense, but it talks about now. The past tense just makes it sound less direct.

### Softening opinions

When you give an opinion, especially a negative one, add a small softener.

- Direct: *This approach is wrong.*
- Softer: *I'm not sure this approach will work.*
- Direct: *This function is too long.*
- Softer: *This function might be a bit long. Maybe we could split it?*

Useful softeners: *a bit, slightly, maybe, might, I think, I'm not sure, it seems.*

```text
I think we might need more tests for the edge cases.
It seems like the cache isn't being cleared on logout.
```

### Don't over-soften

Too much softening can make your message unclear, or make you sound unsure. If something is urgent or important, say it clearly.

- Too soft: *Sorry, I was just maybe wondering if possibly someone could look at prod, if it's not a problem?*
- Clear and polite: *Production is down. Could someone from the platform team look at this now, please?*

## Useful phrases

**Requests**

- **Could you** send me the link?
- **Would you mind** reviewing this today?
- **Would it be possible to** move the meeting to 3pm?
- **I was wondering if** you could help me with the tests.

**Softening opinions**

- **I think** we should…
- **Maybe we could** use a queue here?
- **It might be better to** wait for the next release.
- **I'm not sure** this will scale.

**Small but important words**

- **Just** a quick question. (makes it sound small)
- **When you get a chance.** (no hurry)
- **If that's OK with you.**

**Thanking in advance (politely)**

- **Thanks so much!**
- **That would be really helpful.**

## Common mistakes

**"Send me the file."**
This is grammatically correct but sounds like an order. Use a question: *"**Could you** send me the file?"*

**"Would you mind to review my PR?"**
After *would you mind*, use the *-ing* form: *"Would you mind **reviewing** my PR?"*

**"Would you mind checking the logs?" "Yes, sure!"**
*Yes* means "I mind", so it's a refusal. To agree, say: *"**No, not at all.**"* In real life many people say "Sure!", and that's fine too.

**"I am wondering if you can help me."**
The past form sounds more natural and polite here: *"I **was wondering if you could** help me."*

## Formal vs. casual

- **To a director or client:** "Would it be possible to schedule a short call this week to discuss the timeline?"
- **To a colleague:** "Could we have a quick call this week about the timeline?"
- **To a close teammate:** "Quick call about the timeline sometime this week?"

## Exercise

Look at the last five requests you sent at work in chat or email. Rewrite each one twice:

1. A polite version for someone senior or outside your team.
2. A friendly, short version for a close teammate.

Then find one opinion you gave recently and rewrite it with a softener like *might*, *maybe* or *I'm not sure*.

*Hint:* say your polite versions out loud. If one sounds too long or unclear, remove a softener.

## Recap

- Turn orders into questions: *Could you…? Would you mind…?*
- Use *-ing* after *would you mind*.
- *I was wondering if you could…* is very polite.
- Soften opinions with *might, maybe, a bit, I think*.
- Don't over-soften urgent or important messages.
