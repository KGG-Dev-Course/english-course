## Why this matters

Many developers learn tech words by reading them, not hearing them. So you might write *cache* and *GitHub* perfectly but say them in a way your colleagues don't recognize. In a fast meeting or a job interview, a few unclear words can make people lose track of your whole point.

## The core skill

In this lesson, the pronunciation is written in simple "sounds like" spelling. **CAPITAL letters show the stressed syllable**, the part you say louder and longer. For example, *DAY-ta* means you stress *DAY*.

### Word stress changes everything

In English, getting the stress right matters more than getting every sound perfect. If you stress the wrong syllable, native speakers often don't recognize the word, even if every sound is correct.

- **database**: DAY-ta-bays (not da-ta-BASE)
- **algorithm**: AL-go-rith-um (not al-go-RITH-um)
- **component**: com-POH-nent
- **developer**: de-VEL-o-per
- **architecture**: AR-ki-tek-cher

Some words change stress when they change job in the sentence. *Record* as a noun is **REK-ord**, but as a verb it's **re-KORD**:

```text
Can you check the database RECORD for user 42?
We RECORD every demo so people can watch it later.
```

### Words that don't sound like they look

These words trip up many developers because the spelling is misleading. (*Trip someone up* is a phrasal verb meaning "cause someone to make a mistake".)

- **cache**: sounds like *cash* (one syllable, not "ca-shay" or "catch")
- **queue**: sounds like the letter *Q* (the "ueue" is silent)
- **suite** (test suite): sounds like *sweet*, not *suit*
- **Linux**: LIN-ux (short *i*, like *in*)
- **width**: one syllable, ends with a soft *th*: *width*, not *wid*
- **integer**: IN-te-jer (the *g* sounds like *j*)
- **Kubernetes**: koo-ber-NET-eez
- **Azure**: AZH-er (stress on the first syllable)

### Abbreviations: say letters or say a word?

Some abbreviations are spelled out letter by letter. Others are said as a word. There's no rule you can guess, so learn the common ones.

Spelled out letter by letter:

- **SQL**: S-Q-L or *sequel* (both are common and fine)
- **API**: A-P-I
- **URL**: U-R-L (not *earl*)
- **CI/CD**: C-I-C-D
- **AWS**: A-W-S

Said as a word:

- **JSON**: JAY-son (like the name *Jason*)
- **GUI**: GOO-ee
- **SaaS**: *sass*
- **YAML**: YAM-ul
- **regex**: REJ-ex

Version numbers are usually read digit by digit: *Python 3.12* is "Python three point twelve", and *v2.0.1* is "version two point oh point one".

### Symbols you say out loud

When you read code aloud, share a terminal command, or spell out a URL on a call, you need names for symbols:

```text
/      slash               \      backslash
.      dot                 _      underscore
-      dash / hyphen       #      hash
@      at                  *      asterisk / star
~      tilde               |      pipe
()     brackets / parentheses
{}     curly brackets / braces
[]     square brackets
```

For example, you'd read `api/v2/users_list` as "api slash v two slash users underscore list".

## Useful phrases

**When you're not sure how to say a word**

- **How do you pronounce** this one, *K8s*?
- **Is it** *sequel* **or** S-Q-L **on this team?**
- **I've only seen it written down**, so correct me if I say it wrong.

**When someone doesn't understand a word you said**

- **Let me spell that:** C-A-C-H-E.
- **I mean** the cache, **the one in Redis.**
- **I'll drop it in the chat.** (*drop* here means "quickly post")

**Reading code or commands aloud**

- **Run** npm install **dash dash** save-dev.
- **It's under** src **slash** components.
- **The function takes two arguments in brackets.**

## Common mistakes

**"We need to clear the ca-SHAY."**
Correct: *"We need to clear the **cache**."* (sounds like *cash*)
*Cache* has one syllable. "Ca-shay" sounds like *cachet*, a different word.

**"I'll add it to the kew-ay."**
Correct: *"I'll add it to the **queue**."* (sounds like *Q*)
The letters after the *q* are silent.

**"Please send me the earl."**
Correct: *"Please send me the **U-R-L**."* (or simply *"the link"*)
Most developers spell out *URL* letter by letter.

**"We ran the whole test suit."**
Correct: *"We ran the whole test **suite**."* (sounds like *sweet*)
A *suit* is clothing. A *suite* is a set of things that belong together.

## Formal vs. casual

Pronunciation doesn't change much with formality, but how you check a word does.

- **In an interview or with a client:** "I'll be using Kubernetes, sorry, I'm never sure if I say it right. Is it koo-ber-NET-eez?"
- **With your team:** "Wait, do we say *sequel* or S-Q-L here?"

In both cases, asking is completely normal. Native speakers argue about tech words too. Developers still disagree about whether *GIF* starts with a *g* sound or a *j* sound.

## Exercise

Find five tech words you say often at work: tools, libraries or concepts from your own project.

1. Look up each one in an online dictionary with audio, or find a conference talk on YouTube where a native speaker says it.
2. Write each word with its "sounds like" spelling and mark the stressed syllable in capitals.
3. Record yourself saying a short sentence with each word, like "We store sessions in the cache." Listen and compare.

*Hint:* for tool names like *Nginx* or *PostgreSQL*, check the project's own docs or FAQ. Many projects say how they want their name to be pronounced.

## Recap

- Stress the right syllable. It matters more than perfect sounds.
- Learn the tricky ones: *cache* (cash), *queue* (Q), *suite* (sweet).
- Some abbreviations are spelled out (*API*, *URL*). Others are words (*JSON*, *GUI*).
- Know the names of symbols so you can read paths and commands aloud.
- When you're not sure, ask. It's normal, even for native speakers.
