## Why this matters

Technical discussions are full of opinions: which library to use, how to design an API, when to release. You need to agree in a way that adds something, disagree without starting a fight, and suggest ideas so people actually consider them. The right phrases make this much easier.

## The core skill

### Agreeing, with different strength

Choose how strongly you agree:

- **Strong:** *Absolutely. / I completely agree. / Exactly.*
- **Normal:** *I agree. / That makes sense. / Good point.*
- **Partial:** *I agree up to a point. / That's true, but…*

Agreement is more useful when you add a reason:

```text
I completely agree. We've had three outages from that service this month,
so moving it is worth the effort.
```

### Disagreeing politely

In English, direct disagreement (*No, that's wrong*) can sound rude. The usual pattern is: show you understand, then give your view and your reason.

```text
I see what you mean, but I'm not sure GraphQL is the right choice here.
Most of our clients only need two or three endpoints, so it might add
complexity we don't need.
```

You learned softening words like *might* and *I'm not sure* in lesson 7. They are especially useful here.

Disagree with the idea, not the person. *I'm not sure that will work* is about the idea. *You're wrong* is about the person.

### Making suggestions

Suggestions are often questions. They invite discussion instead of forcing an opinion.

```text
What if we used feature flags instead of a separate branch?
How about splitting the migration into two steps?
Why don't we try it on staging first?
Maybe we could ask the data team for help.
```

Notice the verb forms:

- *What if we* + past simple: *What if we **used**…?* (The past makes it sound like an idea, not a demand.)
- *How about* + *-ing*: *How about **splitting**…?*
- *Why don't we* + base verb: *Why don't we **try**…?*

### Reaching a decision

When a discussion goes in circles, help move it forward.

```text
It sounds like we agree on the API design, but not on the timeline.
Can we decide the timeline on Thursday, when we have the estimates?
```

## Useful phrases

**Agreeing**

- **I'm with** Priya **on this.**
- **That's exactly what I was thinking.**
- **Good point.** **And also,** it would make testing easier.

**Partially agreeing**

- **That's true, but** we also need to think about cost.
- **I agree in principle, but** the timing is difficult.

**Disagreeing**

- **I see what you mean, but** …
- **I'm not convinced that** this will be faster.
- **I have a slightly different view.**
- **I'm not sure about that.** My concern is …

**Suggesting**

- **What if we** added a cache?
- **How about** using the existing service?
- **Have we considered** a simpler approach?
- **One option would be to** ship it behind a flag.

## Common mistakes

**"I am agree with you."**
*Agree* is a verb, so you don't need *am*: *"I **agree** with you."*

**"How about to use Docker?"**
After *how about*, use the *-ing* form: *"How about **using** Docker?"*

**"I don't agree with you, it's a bad idea."**
Correct grammar, but too direct, and *bad idea* sounds personal. Explain: *"**I'm not sure about that, because** it would double our hosting costs."*

**"Why we don't use a queue?"**
In this question, *don't* comes before *we*: *"Why **don't we** use a queue?"*

## Formal vs. casual

- **Architecture review with senior engineers:** "I see the advantages of a microservice here, but I have some concerns about the operational cost. Have we considered keeping it as a module in the monolith for now?"
- **Team discussion:** "Hmm, not sure we need a whole new service. What if we just made it a module for now?"

## Exercise

Think of a recent technical decision on your team, for example a tool, a design or a deadline. Write a short reply for a discussion where you:

1. Partly agree with one idea.
2. Disagree politely with another, with a reason.
3. Make one suggestion, using *What if we…*, *How about…* or *Why don't we…*

*Hint:* say your reply out loud. Does it sound like you're attacking the idea or the person?

## Recap

- Choose the strength of your agreement, and add a reason.
- Disagree by showing you understand, then giving your view and reason.
- Suggest with *What if we…, How about + -ing, Why don't we…*
- *Agree* is a verb: *I agree*, not *I am agree*.
- Help the group move to a decision.
